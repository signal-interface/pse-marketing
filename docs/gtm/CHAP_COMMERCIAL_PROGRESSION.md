# CHAP Commercial Progression

Status: proposed

## Positioning

One CHAP identity provides progressively greater context, tools, and permitted actions. Tier differences must be enforced through capabilities and policy checks, not prompt copy alone.

> The newsletter lets you hear from CHAP. Context Hub lets you ask CHAP. PSE Individual lets you work with CHAP. Organizational PSE lets CHAP support a governed environment.

## Capability matrix

| Tier | Knowledge | Context | Permitted output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Newsletter | Selected approved intelligence | Public issue context | Teaser insight and question |
| Context Hub | Approved PSE intelligence | Member preferences and watchlists | Cited explanations and general checklists |
| Individual | Hub plus approved workspace materials | Practitioner jurisdictions, systems, evidence | Guided reviews, proposed findings, action plans |
| Assessment | Approved assessment evidence | Time-bound organizational scope | Finding explanations and scoped recommendations |
| Team | Shared approved organization context | Roles and team workflows | Review queues and governed collaboration |
| Enterprise | Connected, policy-approved sources | Enterprise policies and systems | Governed orchestration subject to approval |

## Context Hub boundaries

CHAP may:

- cite authoritative sources;
- state jurisdiction and effective date;
- distinguish confirmed requirements from PSE interpretation;
- identify uncertainty;
- recommend approved resources;
- generate general checklists.

CHAP may not:

- answer from unapproved drafts;
- represent legal or tax advice;
- ingest employer payroll data at the Context Hub tier;
- produce organization-specific findings;
- execute payroll or system changes;
- conceal uncertainty or source conflict.

## Governance requirement

Higher tiers widen available context and workflow scope. They do not silently widen authority. Consequential actions require a policy decision and an auditable event.
